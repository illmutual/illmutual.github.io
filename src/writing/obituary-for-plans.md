
Plans died Thursday morning. They were only twenty-four hours old. Plans had dreamed of becoming dinner. Before that they had been coffee, a movie, class, a ticket bought without refund insurance. They believed intently in later.

Their final hours were spent beside a glass of water and an emesis bag on the nightstand.

The body woke first. It usually does.

By nine, Plans had received a notification: So sorry! I’m not feeling well.

Plans had heard this before. There are only so many ways to explain that Body has backed out.

Sometimes Body sweats through the sheets before it has opened its eyes. Sometimes it wakes up with its heart already pounding. Sometimes there is enough warning to make it to the bathroom. Sometimes there isn’t. Body can empty itself and keep going. It folds over the toilet and spits strings of saliva into yellow water. Body is especially productive in the morning.

Plans did not survive the morning.

They had many prior close calls. Once, Plans made it all the way into the shower before Body went down. Shampoo ran into its eyes. Another time, Plans got dressed, put on shoes, found the keys, and stood very still beside the front door before Plans woke up investigating the ceiling.

Like the many before them, Plans died in small spaces. Beside the bed. On the bathroom tile. Half-dressed on the floor. Some died with one shoe on.

Through it all, Plans were always optimistic. Plans made sure to set alarms. They even washed their hair the night before.

Plans are survived by Plans. There are several more this week.
